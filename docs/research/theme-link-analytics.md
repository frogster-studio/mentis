# Which cookieless analytics tool for the Theme Link

Research for [#91](https://github.com/frogster-studio/mentis/issues/91), map [#87](https://github.com/frogster-studio/mentis/issues/87). Facts gathered 2026-11-20 from CNIL and vendor pages; no account was created anywhere.

## Answer

**PostHog Cloud EU, in `cookieless_mode: "always"`, with everything but pageviews and our four events switched off.** It is the only candidate that meets all four constraints on documented facts: no cookies or storage, hosted in Frankfurt, free up to 1M events a month, custom events with properties, and real funnels that break down by `slug`.

- **Runner-up: Plausible Business**, if paying is acceptable. It is the cleanest fit for CNIL (EU company, daily salt, no view of a single visitor), but it is **not free**: custom properties and funnels need Business, $19/month at 10k or $39 at 100k billable events.
- **Umami Cloud** is free and has properties and funnels, but it is a US company that documents no EU region you can choose. Its Sessions view also lists individual visitors.
- **Out:** Vercel Web Analytics (no custom events on Hobby, no funnels on any plan), Pirsch (no free plan), Simple Analytics (the free plan has no events, metadata or goals).

**CNIL:** the exemption from consent is now self-assessed. The CNIL keeps **no list** of exempt tools. It publishes criteria and a self-assessment form that vendors fill in, and the publisher (us) stays liable for how the tool is configured. None of the six vendors has published a CNIL self-assessment that I could find. So whichever tool we pick, the spec has to include our own configuration checklist (below) and an opt-out link on the privacy page.

Volume: a few thousand sessions × (1 pageview + up to 4 events, plus replays) ≈ 15–30k events a month. Every vendor here bills custom events like pageviews.

## 1. CNIL: what makes measurement exempt from consent

Sources: [Cookies : solutions pour les outils de mesure d'audience](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs/regles/cookies-solutions-pour-les-outils-de-mesure-daudience), [self-assessment tool, July 2025 (PDF)](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf), [Que dit la loi ?](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs/que-dit-la-loi).

- **Being cookieless does not exempt a tool by itself.** Article 82 covers any "traceur": the page names fingerprinting ("le résultat du calcul d'une empreinte unique du terminal") and identifiers generated by software. A hash of IP + User-Agent is a traceur, and the exemption conditions still apply to it.
- **No list of approved tools.** The audience-measurement page names no solution. It points vendors to the self-assessment form and forbids them to describe a tool as "certifiée" or "validée par la CNIL". The self-assessment "does not bind the CNIL", and both the publisher and the vendor can be held liable.
- **Criteria in the self-assessment form** (where the form names a specific technical measure, that measure is a recommendation, and the vendor may justify an equivalent):
  - **Purpose:** audience measurement only. Marketing uses, including "mesure de performance des canaux de conversion" and campaign measurement, must be off by default. So must any cohort used to show different content (A/B tests, feature flags).
  - **Minimised data:** HTTP headers reduced (major OS or browser version). **At most three kinds of event:** presence on a page, use of a feature (button or link click, with its label), and load, scroll or time-on-page statistics. Our four events are all "use of a feature".
  - **No tracking outside the site:** no import of CRM ids, **UTMs or campaign ids**. Referrer reduced to its host. No integration with third-party tools.
  - **Identifier:** if the tool fingerprints, the hash must include **a component specific to the site** (for example the domain) **and a time component**, so the fingerprint has a limited life. If an IP is used, at most city-level location, then truncated.
  - **Anonymous output:** reports are aggregated (the form suggests rounding to the nearest ten, or a documented analysis of anonymity). No combination of filters may single out a user. **"Aucun suivi de la navigation d'un utilisateur unique"**: session replay is the example the form gives.
  - **Vendor as processor:** an Article 28 DPA, no pooling of raw data across customers, **no reuse for the vendor's own purposes**.
  - **Right to object:** a clickable opt-out in the site's privacy policy, made to last (an opt-out cookie, or a fingerprint added to a blocklist).
- The general page also recommends keeping data at most 25 months and checking for transfers outside the EU.

What this means for the Theme Link:
- The privacy page needs an **opt-out link**. The map already plans CGU and privacy links on results.
- Don't collect UTMs, and reduce the referrer to its host.
- Don't use any feature that shows one visitor's own trail of events.

### PostHog Cloud EU

- **Cookieless:** `cookieless_mode: "always"` means "PostHog sets no cookies and uses no session or local storage, with user identity handled by a privacy-preserving hash generated on PostHog's servers… no cookie banner needed". It must also be switched on in the project settings, or events are dropped ([JS config](https://posthog.com/docs/libraries/js/config)). The hash covers team id, a daily salt (deleted once the day is processed), IP, user agent and hostname ([cookieless tutorial](https://posthog.com/tutorials/cookieless-tracking)). That matches the CNIL's "site component + time component". The IP is stripped first, so there is no GeoIP or bot detection. Weekly and monthly uniques are inflated (one visitor on 7 days counts as 7).
- **EU:** "PostHog Cloud EU… hosted on servers based in Frankfurt". IP capture is off by default for new EU projects ([GDPR guide](https://posthog.com/docs/privacy/gdpr-compliance)).
- **Price:** the Free plan includes 1M product-analytics events a month, 1 project and 1-year retention, with no credit card. Funnels are part of Product analytics ([pricing](https://posthog.com/pricing)).
- **Events:** `posthog.capture("finished_quiz_session", { slug })`.
- **Funnels:** steps are events, each filterable by event properties, with a breakdown by event property and a conversion window ([funnels](https://posthog.com/docs/product-analytics/funnels)).
- **CNIL burden on us:** PostHog is a product suite, so the defaults must be cut back in the config.
  - Set `autocapture: false`, `capture_dead_clicks: false`, `rageclick: false`, `capture_heatmaps: false`, `disable_session_recording: true`, `disable_surveys: true`, `advanced_disable_flags: true`, `person_profiles: "never"`.
  - Never call `identify`.
  - Use `before_send` to drop UTMs and reduce `$referrer` to its host.
  - Its event and person views can show one anonymous visitor's day of events. That conflicts with the "no single-user navigation" criterion, so the team must agree not to use them. This residual risk does not exist with Plausible.
- **Loads in Expo web:** the npm package `posthog-js` (1.453.0). Initialise it in a web-only module (`*.web.ts`, so the native bundle never imports it) with `api_host: "https://eu.i.posthog.com"`, client-side only. The client must not run during static rendering in Node. No `+html.tsx` is needed.

### Plausible

- **Cookieless:** the identifier is a hash of a daily salt + website domain + IP + User-Agent, and the salt is "rotated and deleted every 24 hours". The tracker writes no cookies or local storage. It only reads a `plausible_ignore` flag for opt-out ([data policy](https://plausible.io/data-policy)). "Visitor data does not leave the EU." That is the closest match to the CNIL form among the six.
- **Price:** no free plan, only a 30-day trial. Starter is $9 at 10k and includes goals and custom events. **Custom properties and funnels are Business only**: $19 at 10k, $39 at 100k, $59 at 200k (price table on [plausible.io](https://plausible.io/)). Custom events count as billable pageviews ([custom events](https://plausible.io/docs/custom-event-goals)). A free self-hosted Community Edition exists, but it needs a server of our own.
- **Events:** `track("Download", { props: { slug, platform } })`. Up to 30 properties, scalar values only ([custom props](https://plausible.io/docs/custom-props/introduction)).
- **Funnels:** 2–8 steps from pages or goals, in sequential, flexible or strict mode. A property value can only become a step through a property goal. The documented funnel filters don't include custom properties ([funnels](https://plausible.io/docs/funnel-analysis)).
- **A cheaper path, inferred and not verified:** the Theme Link URL already holds the slug (`/<slug>`), and events record their page. So on Starter, filtering by page plus event names such as `Download iOS` could give per-slug counts with no custom properties. You would lose the funnel view and keep only conversion counts.
- **Loads in Expo web:** the npm package `@plausible-analytics/tracker` (0.4.7), browser only: `init({ domain })`, then `track(...)`. SPA pageviews are captured automatically ([README](https://github.com/plausible/analytics/tree/master/tracker/npm_package)). Alternatively, a plain `POST https://plausible.io/api/event` ([Events API](https://plausible.io/docs/events-api)).

### Umami Cloud

- **Cookieless:** the identifier is a hash of website id + IP + User-Agent + a salt that rotates **monthly** ([metric definitions](https://docs.umami.is/docs/metric-definitions)). That is a time component, but a long one.
- **EU:** the pricing page says Cloud servers are "in the US and EU". No page says how to pick the EU or where a given account lives ([pricing](https://umami.is/pricing), [docs](https://docs.umami.is/docs/cloud)). Umami is a San Francisco company. Its DPA relies on SCCs, and its subprocessors include AWS, Cloudflare and Vercel in the US ([DPA](https://umami.is/dpa), [subprocessors](https://umami.is/subprocessors)). **"EU-hosted" can't be shown from its documents.**
- **Price:** Hobby is free with 100k events a month, 1 website and 6-month retention, and includes custom events, event properties, funnels and journeys. Pro is $20/month.
- **Events:** `umami.track("finished_quiz_session", { slug })`. Up to 50 properties, strings up to 500 characters ([tracker functions](https://docs.umami.is/docs/tracker-functions)). The Properties tab breaks an event down by property value ([event data](https://docs.umami.is/docs/event-data)).
- **Funnels:** steps are URLs, URL prefixes or events, with a window in minutes between steps. Filtering by property is not documented ([funnel](https://docs.umami.is/docs/funnel)).
- **CNIL:** the Sessions view lists each visitor's country, browser, OS "and the pages they viewed", with an activity history per visitor ([sessions](https://docs.umami.is/docs/sessions)). That is the "single-user navigation" the form excludes.
- **Loads in Expo web:** a script tag only, with no official npm package. Expo only applies `+html.tsx` with `web.output: "static"`; the static-vs-single choice belongs to #88. Alternatively, a plain `POST https://gateway.umami.is/api/send`: no auth, a `User-Agent` header is required ([sending stats](https://docs.umami.is/docs/api/sending-stats)).

### Vercel Web Analytics — out

- Hobby: 50k events a month, but **custom events are "available on Enterprise and Pro plans"** only. Properties per event: Pro 2, Plus ($10/month add-on) 8, with values up to 255 characters ([pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/analytics/limits-and-pricing), [custom events](https://vercel.com/docs/analytics/custom-events)). Two properties (slug + platform) would just fit on Pro.
- No funnel feature: the dashboard offers panels, filters and a CSV export ([using Web Analytics](https://vercel.com/docs/analytics/using-web-analytics)).
- Visitors are "identified by a hash created from the incoming request", and the session is discarded after 24 h. The storage region is not stated ([privacy](https://vercel.com/docs/analytics/privacy-policy)).
- **Loads in Expo web:** `@vercel/analytics` (2.0.1) with `inject()`. It only works on a Vercel deployment.

### Pirsch — out (not free)

- Hosted in Germany. Hash of IP + User-Agent + date + a salt per site, so recognition lasts 24 h. No cookies ([privacy](https://docs.pirsch.io/privacy)).
- No free plan, only a 30-day trial. Standard is $6 and Plus $12 at 10k. **Funnels are Plus only**, up to 8 steps with event and tag filters ([pricing](https://pirsch.io/pricing), [funnels](https://docs.pirsch.io/advanced/funnels)).
- `pirsch("name", { meta: {...} })`: up to 20 string pairs. Events count toward the pageview quota ([events](https://docs.pirsch.io/advanced/events)).

### Simple Analytics — out

- Hosted in the Netherlands. "Never collect information that could be used to fingerprint a user." Uniqueness comes from the referrer, and sessions "don't use an identifier" ([pricing FAQ](https://www.simpleanalytics.com/pricing), [unique visits](https://docs.simpleanalytics.com/explained/unique-visits)). This is the safest model for CNIL, but it can't really chain several events from one visitor.
- The free plan: 5 sites, 30-day history, badge required. The pricing page's embedded plan data marks goals, events and metadata **off** on Free and on in the €20/month plan.
- `sa_event("click_download", { filename })` ([metadata](https://docs.simpleanalytics.com/metadata)). Goals can have steps ([goals](https://docs.simpleanalytics.com/goals)).

## 3. Reading the funnel

The four events happen on one page, in one visit. The proposed names are `question_attempted` (fired once, on the first Question), `finished_quiz_session`, `download_tap` (with `platform`: `ios` / `android` / `desktop_qr`) and `share_tap`. Every event carries `slug`.

- **PostHog:** one Funnel insight `question_attempted → finished_quiz_session → download_tap`, broken down by `slug`, plus a second funnel ending on `share_tap`. The download step can also be broken down by `platform`. A conversion window of about 1 hour fits. Replays fire `finished_quiz_session` again, and the funnel counts each user once. A Trends insight shows the raw count of finishes.
- **Plausible Business:** the four events become goals, and a funnel is built from them. Per-slug numbers come from filtering the dashboard by the `slug` property, or from a property goal per step.
- **Umami:** a funnel on the three events. Per-slug counts come from the Properties tab of each event.

## 4. Side remark: reuse in the native app later

- **PostHog** has a React Native SDK (`posthog-react-native`), so the same project and the same event names would work.
- **Plausible** documents its Events API for mobile apps, sent from the device.
- **Umami's** `/api/send` works from any client that sends a `User-Agent`.
- **Vercel Web Analytics** is web only.
